The lesson is a scene in the story

Nobody stops the story to explain things. An idea turns up when a character needs it. On this page from What She Left Behind, a retired nurse explains her investment club using a tin of sweets.

What She Left Behind, chapter ten

Now. Say you had to put all your money into one sweet in this tin. Which one would you choose?

picking one company is risky

I wouldn't, I say.

No, says Bernadette. Neither would I. I nursed for forty-one years, I'm not stupid, but I don't know which firm is going to do best next year. Nobody does. So I don't pick. I buy the tin.

spreading it out: that's diversifying

It's a fund, she says. Hundreds of people put their money in together, and the fund buys a little of everything on a list. The list is called an index.

and that's an index fund

Nobody's paid to be clever. That's why it's cheap. The orange costs us about a fifth of one per cent a year to own.

One lesson, three different books

That was index funds for a reader who loves dark, funny family fiction. Here's the same lesson written for three other readers. Pick the one that sounds like you.

a cosy crime at a bowls club

“So you don’t pick the winners,” said Marjorie.

“Nobody picks anything,” said Clive, who had been a bank manager for thirty-one years and still dressed like one. “That’s the beauty of it. You buy a sliver of every company on the list. All five hundred. The good, the bad, and the one that makes those dreadful scented candles.”

“Even the candles?”

“Especially the candles. Some will go bust and some will go through the roof. You don’t need to know which, because you own both. And nobody clever is paid to choose, so it costs about the price of a biscuit a year.” He paused. “A cheap biscuit.”

Marjorie wrote it down. “And it can’t go down?”

Clive looked at her over his glasses. “Marjorie. Everything can go down.”

a quiet love story in Dublin

They were lying on his bed with the window open. Outside, someone was failing to start a car.

You don’t have to choose, Cian said. That’s the whole point. You just buy a tiny bit of every company on the list. It’s called an index fund.

She turned this over. It seemed too simple, like a trick she would be embarrassed to have missed.

So no one is being clever, she said.

No. That’s why it’s cheap. Clever costs money.

And if they all go down?

Then it goes down, he said. Then you wait.

He said it the way he said most things, as if he had already accepted the worst version and wasn’t frightened of it. She wanted, suddenly, to be like that about something.

a romantasy with a dragon’s hoard

The hoard filled the cavern: crowns, swords, a single golden spoon.

“You could have taken the crown,” Kael said, leaning against the rock with his wings folded, infuriating.

“And if the crown is cursed, I’m cursed with it.” I opened my hand. One coin from every kingdom on the map. Copper, silver, gold, the green ones from the drowned realm. “If one kingdom falls, I still have the rest. I don’t have to guess which king survives the war.”

“Nobody’s ever stolen like that.”

“Nobody’s ever been this poor. It costs me nothing to carry.” I closed my fingers. “And if the whole world burns, it burns with it.”

His mouth curved. “Then we’ll make sure it doesn’t.”

Same lesson every time: an index fund buys a little of every company on a list, nobody is paid to pick, so it’s cheap, and it can still fall.

Questions

What can I learn?

Most things that can be explained in a good conversation: money and investing, how AI works, a slice of history, science basics like the body, space or the climate, running a small business. Tell us your subject and we'll say honestly if a story can teach it well.

Is it accurate?

Every book is built on a checked outline of the facts it teaches, and each chapter is checked against it before you see it. It's still a story, not professional advice. Money books never recommend particular companies or products, and the figures in them are made-up examples.

Who writes it?

We write each story with the help of AI tools, from your answers and a story design we plan first. A person reads every book before it's delivered, and you see chapter one before the rest is finished.

Can it be dark? Can it avoid things?

Yes to both. You choose how dark it can go, from gentle to properly messy, and list anything to avoid. We never include anything on that list.

Can I listen instead of reading?

Yes. Chapter one is read aloud for everyone. Add the audiobook for $6.99 when you pay and every chapter is, so you can switch between reading and listening whenever you like. Members get the audiobook included.

How long does it take?

About four days. We design your story and send you chapter one within 24 hours. Once you're happy with it, the whole book follows within three days.

Will it arrive in time for Christmas?

Yes, if you order by 16 December while places are open. It arrives by 23 December, or we can schedule the link for Christmas morning. There's nothing to wrap, so make a card to hand over on the day.

Will you use real names?

No. Everyone in your story gets a name we make up, and places stay at the level of a town, city or country. Your own words appear only in the dedication, if you write one.

Will my story be shared?

It's yours alone for six months. After that, a library edition may join the Learned by Heart library for other readers, with the dedication removed and anything that could point to you changed. You can untick this when you order, or opt out by email any time before then.

What do you do with what I tell you?

We use your answers only to write the book, and delete them 60 days after delivery unless you ask us to keep them. Please don't include health or other sensitive details. The privacy note has the details.

What if I don't like it?

Tell us after chapter one and we'll change it. And if you're not happy with the finished book, email us within 30 days of getting it and we'll refund everything you paid. You don't need to give a reason.

Can I get it printed?

Printed hardbacks are coming later. Every book can be printed once they are.
